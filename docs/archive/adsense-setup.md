Vignette ads showing up when a user clicks links or buttons are triggered via Auto Ads. It is fully automated by Google, but you can control its intensity using the Ad Load slider (which is what you might be remembering as a choice between "minimum" or "maximum" / "max"). [1, 2, 3] 
## 🛠️ How it Works: Auto Ads vs. Ad Load

* It is on Auto: Vignettes are an "Overlay format" nested under [Google AdSense Auto Ads](https://support.google.com/adsense/answer/9261805?hl=en). Google uses machine learning to automatically detect when a user clicks an eligible internal hyperlink or button on your utility site, popping up the full-screen ad between those page loads. [1, 3, 4, 5, 6] 
* The "Max" vs. "Min" Aspect (Ad Load Slider): While you cannot manually code where a vignette triggers, you can go into your AdSense Auto Ads settings and adjust the Ad Load slider. Moving it toward Max lets Google show vignettes and other ads more frequently, while moving it toward Min tightens frequency caps so users do not get annoyed. [1, 3, 7] 
* Frequency Controls: AdSense lets you set an exact frequency cap for vignettes (e.g., limiting them to show only once every 10 minutes per user) to keep your utility tools usable. [8, 9] 

## ⚠️ A Major Warning for Utility Sites
If you run a utility site, be extremely careful with Auto Vignette ads.
Because utility sites rely heavily on clicks (like clicking "Convert File", "Calculate", or "Download"), Google's Auto Ads algorithm can easily misinterpret these functional tool buttons as standard navigation links. [4, 10] 

* The Problem: A user clicks "Download PDF", but instead of getting their file, a massive full-screen vignette ad blocks them. This destroys the user experience and can lead to accidental ad clicks. [2, 11] 
* The Fix: If Auto Ads start breaking your tool buttons, you can add rel="amzn-no-ads" or specialized data attributes to your HTML buttons to tell Google's automated system to ignore those specific button clicks. [4]

No, you do not need to explicitly render content as links to show drawer ads (known as anchor ads) or vignette ads. Google AdSense completely handles this automatically via its [Auto Ads machine learning system](https://support.google.com/adsense/answer/9261307?hl=en). [1, 2, 3, 4, 5] 
However, for a utility site, understanding exactly how Google handles these two different "overlay" formats is crucial to preventing user experience breakdowns:
## 1. Anchor Ads ("Drawer Ads")

* How it works: These are the sticky ads that slide up or down from the edge of the user's screen.
* Do you need links? No. They require absolutely zero user actions, links, or button clicks to appear. As long as a user opens your page and scrolls or interacts with the screen, the anchor drawer will automatically slide into view. [2, 6] 

## 2. Vignette Ads (Full-Screen Intersitial Ads)

* How it works: These are the full-screen ads that pop up when a user transitions between actions.
* Google's Automatic Triggers: Historically, vignettes only triggered when a user clicked a standard HTML text link (<a> tag). However, Google AdSense automatically expanded vignette triggers. Google automatically monitors and triggers vignettes when users try to navigate away, use the browser's back button, or perform standard interactions—meaning you do not need to rewrite your site's code into navigation links just to get them to show. [1, 7, 8, 9, 10] 

## 3. Intent-Driven "Ad Intent" Links

* How it works: This is a separate Auto Ads format where Google scans the plain text on your site and turns specific words into hyper-linked keywords. [3, 11] 
* Do you need to code links? No. Google will automatically scan your user manuals or tool descriptions and convert ordinary text strings into contextual links that open a pop-up ad dialog when clicked. [11] 

## ⚠️ Critical Configuration Rule for Your Utility Site
Because you do not need to explicitly render links, Google will guess where to deploy these ads. For utility sites, this automated behavior can become highly disruptive: [11] 

* The Risk: If you have an AJAX-driven calculation button that updates numbers on the screen without changing the URL, Google's Auto Ads may treat that click as a transition event and block the user with a full-screen vignette. [1, 9] 
* The Fix: If Google starts misbehaving on your utility buttons, you do not need to remove Auto Ads entirely. You can selectively block vignettes on specific buttons by adding the data-google-vignette="false" tag directly to your critical HTML elements. [5, 12]

Yes, you can absolutely partner with other ad networks alongside Google AdSense on the same website. In fact, combining AdSense with other platforms is the industry-standard strategy used by top-earning utility sites to maximize their revenue. [1, 2, 3] 
Google’s official policy explicitly allows you to run competing ad networks on the same page, provided the layout does not look spammy and the alternative ads do not mimic Google's formatting. [4, 5] 
Here are the best strategies to successfully mix and match networks for a high-utility website.
------------------------------
## Strategy 1: Header Bidding (The Advanced & Lucrative Way)
Instead of running AdSense side-by-side with another network, you can look into Header Bidding. This is the exact strategy used by high-earning utility sites like the one you mentioned. [6] 

* How it works: You partner with a monetization platform (like Ezoic, Snigel, or Monumetric). They install a single piece of code on your site that connects your ad spaces to dozens of global ad exchanges including Google. [7, 8, 9, 10, 11] 
* The Auction: Every time a user loads your page, Google and all the other networks bid against each other in real-time for your ad space. The highest bidder wins and displays their ad. [12] 
* Why it wins: This eliminates guesswork. If AdSense is willing to pay $2.00 for a spot, but another network bids $3.50, you automatically get the higher payout.

## Strategy 2: The Format-Splitting Setup (The Easy DIY Way)
If you want to manually manage your ad setup, you can split your ad formats across different providers based on what each network does best. [13] 

* Google AdSense for Clean Display Banners: Place 1 or 2 standard AdSense responsive display banners at the top or bottom of your utility workspace. Google has the cleanest, safest banner ads that will not scare away users. [14] 
* Adsterra or PropellerAds for High-Yield Intersitials: Use an alternative network specifically to handle your Vignettes (Interstitials) or Push Notifications. Alternative networks are often far less restrictive with pop-up frequency and can yield a massive RPM on utility button clicks compared to AdSense. [15, 16] 
* Infolinks for Text-Based Ads: If your utility page has a user guide or FAQ section at the bottom, use Infolinks to monetize the text. It will inject hyper-targeted contextual link ads without interfering with your main AdSense banners. [17, 18, 19] 

------------------------------
## ⚠️ Critical Guardrails for Utility Sites
When combining networks, you must follow these rules to avoid getting your Google AdSense account banned: [20] 
## 1. Keep Your ads.txt File Updated [21] 
Every network you join will give you a line of code to add to your website's ads.txt file (e.g., google.com, pub-xxxxxxxxxxxx, DIRECT...). You must list every single network you partner with in this file. If a network tries to sell an ad on your site and they are not listed in your ads.txt, your revenue will drop to zero. [22, 23, 24] 
## 2. Watch Your Cumulative "Ad Load"
Do not overload your page. If you have 3 AdSense banners, a pop-up from Adsterra, and an anchor drawer from Ezoic all loading at once, Google will flag your site for "Low Value Content / Aggressive Monetization" and disable your account. Ensure your actual utility tool remains the primary focus of the screen. [25] 
## 3. Ban Redirection Ads
If you use lower-tier alternative networks alongside AdSense, go into their dashboards and explicitly turn off "Auto-Redirects" or "Malicious Ads." If an alternative ad network hijacks your user's browser and forces them to an external app store or betting site, Google will penalize your domain.
