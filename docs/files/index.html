What people use a full screen color for

White screen for lighting & photos

Use your phone or laptop as a soft white light source for selfies, product photos, or as a plain white background behind you on a video call.

Black screen to save battery & focus

A pure black screen uses less power on OLED displays and works as a screensaver, a distraction-free background, or a quick way to darken a room's ambient glow.

Zoom & video call backgrounds

Fill a second monitor or window with a solid color to use as a clean virtual-background substitute or to bounce even light onto your face.

Monitor & dead-pixel testing

Cycle through white, black, red, green and blue full screens to check for dead pixels, screen burn-in, uniformity, and color accuracy.

Green & blue screen for streaming

No physical backdrop? Display solid green or blue full screen on a spare monitor or tablet for quick chroma-key tests.

Calming color & night light

Warm amber or red full-screen light is gentler on the eyes at night than a bright white display.

Frequently asked questions

How do I make my screen fully white?

Tap the white swatch above. Your browser fills the entire screen edge-to-edge with pure white (#FFFFFF). Tap anywhere or press Escape to exit.

Will this work on my phone?

Yes — it works on any modern phone, tablet, or desktop browser. On iPhone Safari, allow full screen when prompted for the smoothest edge-to-edge result.

Can I use a custom color, not just the presets?

Yes. Use the custom color picker above to choose any hex color and go full screen with it.

Does this drain my battery?

A black full screen typically uses less power on OLED/AMOLED screens since those pixels turn off. White or bright colors use more power, similar to normal bright browsing.
